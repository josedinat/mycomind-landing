The problem is not memory. It is the switching.

Mentoring one founder is easy to hold in your head. Following eight or ten is a different job. Each has a distinct arc — a pivot in March, a co-founder conversation you promised to revisit, a hiring decision that was still open last time. None of it is complicated on its own. What makes it hard is that you switch between them every few days, and each switch costs a reconstruction.

So the fifteen minutes before a session get spent skimming old notes, or the session opens with "remind me where we landed on..." — which puts the work of remembering onto the person you are meant to be helping.

Why the usual tools do not fix it

  • A CRM assumes a pipeline with stages. Mentoring is not a pipeline, and the useful context never fits the fields. So it goes in a free-text box, or nowhere.
  • Session notes require you to write them, which happens reliably in quiet weeks and stops in busy ones — exactly the weeks that generate the most context.
  • A meeting recorder gives you a transcript per session. Ten sessions later you have ten documents and still no answer to "where does this stand?".

The common failure is the same in all three: they store what happened and leave you to reconstruct what it means. That gap has a name — cognitive continuity — and it is the actual problem.

What it looks like instead

1

Record the session, or talk for two minutes after it

Live Capture runs during the call and picks up the conversation, including the other participants. If you would rather not record, dictate a couple of minutes afterwards — it works the same way. Documents and decks can be dropped in too.

2

It files itself against the right founder

Decisions, action items and open questions are pulled out automatically, and the session joins everything else about that person. No tagging, no folders, no naming convention to keep up.

3

Next time, open the workspace instead of your notes

Where things stand today, the milestones that got them here, which decisions are in effect, which were superseded, and what is still unresolved. Ten seconds instead of fifteen minutes.

One workspace per person you follow — current without being maintained

The part that only works when you follow several

Once each founder has their own project, a single question can be asked across all of them at once. Search “go-to-market” and you get one reading per company: who shifted approach and when, who has not touched it in two months, what is still unresolved.

This is the view that does not exist anywhere else, because it requires the per-person context to already be structured. It is useful to you as a mentor, and it is the view a program director normally has to request as a status update from everyone.

Seeing a founder change direction

Because positions are kept with their dates rather than overwritten, a change of direction stays visible instead of silently replacing what came before. You can see that a founder held one view of their ICP for four months and now holds another — which is often the most useful thing to raise in the next session, and the easiest thing to miss when you only see the current state.

It is descriptive, never a score. Changing your mind is usually evidence of learning; the point is to notice it, not to grade it.

Questions mentors ask

How do mentors keep context between sessions?
By capturing the session itself rather than writing notes about it. The recording becomes a memory that joins everything else about that mentee, and the workspace for that person stays current on its own — so the next session opens with where things stand, what was decided and what is still open.
Do I have to write session notes?
No. You record the conversation, or dictate a couple of minutes afterwards, and the decisions, action items and open questions are extracted automatically. There are no fields to fill in and no folders to file into.
Can I keep each mentee separate?
Yes. Each mentee or company gets its own project, and material only enters a project when you explicitly share it there. Your personal memory stays personal by default.
Is what my mentees tell me kept confidential?
Your memory is private by default and is never used to train AI models. Nothing is shared with an organization or another person unless you share it, and a shared memory can be withdrawn at any time.
