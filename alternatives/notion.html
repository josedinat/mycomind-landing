Notion organizes your docs.
My-CoMind preserves your thinking.

Notion is an exceptional tool for wikis, databases, and structured notes. But when your work depends on tracking how decisions evolved and maintaining continuity across weeks or months, you need something different.

What Notion does exceptionally well

Notion is a powerhouse for teams that need a centralized knowledge base. Its strengths are undeniable:

  • Flexible databases — relational tables, kanban boards, and calendars that adapt to any workflow
  • Team wikis — a single source of truth for company documentation and SOPs
  • Templates and customization — build almost any system you can imagine
  • Collaborative editing — real-time co-writing with comments and mentions

If your primary need is a structured knowledge base with rich formatting, Notion is hard to beat.

Where Notion falls short for cognitive work

The challenge starts when you need to answer questions like:

  • "Why did we decide to pivot the pricing model three months ago?"
  • "What were the open questions from the last mentor session with this startup?"
  • "How has our go-to-market strategy shifted since April?"

In Notion, these answers are buried in pages you have to manually find, read, and synthesize. The tool stores information — it doesn't surface the evolution of thinking behind it.

The three gaps

1. No automatic connection — Related notes stay in separate pages unless you manually link them. After 50+ meetings, those links break.

2. No decision timeline — You can see what was decided, but not when the position shifted, how long it held, or what triggered the change.

3. Search is keyword-based — You must remember exact terms to find what you need. You can't ask "why did we decide that?" and get a sourced answer.

When to choose My-CoMind over Notion

My-CoMind isn't a replacement for Notion's wiki capabilities. It's a complement — or a switch — when your work involves:

  • High-stakes decisions that evolve over months and need a clear audit trail
  • Multiple clients or mentees where context switching is expensive
  • Cross-functional teams where leadership needs visibility without status reports
  • Consulting or advisory work where you can't afford to rebuild context every session
